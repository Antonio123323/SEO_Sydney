Stock and Crypto Apps in the UK (2026): How to Compare Both

Copied

Quick answer: can one UK app offer stocks and crypto?

Yes, a single interface may give access to share dealing and crypto-related services. However, a stock and crypto trading app does not necessarily provide the same type of account, ownership rights or protections for both products. The important question is what each feature actually does.

This guide helps readers compare stock and crypto apps UK users may encounter. It is educational information, not a ranking, an endorsement or personalised investment advice. Features and eligibility can change, so check the relevant provider terms before making any decision.

What does “stocks and crypto in one app” actually mean?

Some apps display shares and digital assets in a single dashboard but route them through different legal entities, partners or account agreements. A share dealing service might allow ownership of company shares, while a crypto feature could involve holding tokens with a custodian or obtaining price exposure through another product.

When reviewing apps for stocks and crypto UK readers should look for the product description rather than relying on a familiar brand name or a convenient interface. Check whether you own shares, hold a derivative, receive a beneficial interest or access a cryptoasset service. These distinctions affect your rights and the risks you take.

A provider may also offer exchange-traded products that track crypto prices. Such products are not the same as owning transferable cryptocurrency, and access may be restricted for certain retail users.

Compare the two sides of the account separately

For stocks, ask which markets and instruments are supported, whether fractional shares are available, how corporate actions work, and what happens when you want to move your holdings elsewhere. For crypto, check which assets and networks are supported, whether you can send tokens to an external wallet, and who controls the private keys.

An investing apps for stocks and crypto search often brings up services with very different business models. A useful comparison should cover ownership, GBP funding, full cost, custody, transfers and the conditions that apply to UK customers—not just the number of assets shown on a homepage.

GBP funding and moving money

Before you buy stocks and crypto in one app, find out how deposits work for each service. GBP funding may involve bank transfer, payment cards or an internal cash balance, with different steps for share dealing and crypto purchases.

Check whether funds can move freely between account sections, whether an exchange rate is applied, and whether the bank or provider imposes additional checks. Account verification, deposit processing and withdrawal availability can vary by customer and payment method.

If bank compatibility is a concern, our guide to UK bank policies for crypto payments explains questions worth asking before a transfer.

Fees: look beyond the headline commission

Full costs can include trading fees, a spread between buy and sell quotes, foreign-exchange conversion, custody or account charges, and withdrawal or network fees. Share dealing and crypto transactions may have separate pricing schedules.

A zero-commission message does not mean every transaction is cost-free. Compare the amount paid, the quantity received and the conditions for moving or selling the asset. For a crypto and stock trading app UK comparison, read both sets of product terms instead of assuming one fee schedule covers everything.

Custody, ownership and withdrawals

Stock ownership and crypto custody should be examined independently. Shares may be held through a nominee arrangement, while cryptoassets may remain in a custodial account. The availability of asset transfers, withdrawal limits and recovery procedures can differ.

Ask whether you can transfer shares to another broker and whether crypto transfers to an external address are permitted. If crypto withdrawals are unavailable, holding an in-app balance may not be equivalent to holding assets in a self-custody wallet.

To understand the difference between custodial and self-custody options, see our guide to crypto wallet types for UK readers.

UK regulation and consumer protections

Do not assume that a provider being regulated for one activity means every product in its app receives identical protection. Different services may have different regulatory treatment, legal entities and complaint routes. The FCA register can help identify certain authorised or registered firms, but registration is not a guarantee of safety or product performance.

FSCS protection depends on the activity, firm and eligibility; it should not be assumed to cover losses on cryptocurrency or falls in market value. Read the provider’s disclosures for each product and check official UK guidance when a claim about protection seems unclear.

Rules concerning cryptoassets and investment products can change. This article avoids fixed claims about who may access a specific product and does not describe any platform as “FCA approved” for all activities.

Tax records and different asset types

Transactions involving shares and cryptoassets may create different reporting considerations. Keep records of purchases, sales, transfers, fees and relevant valuations. Do not assume that moving between app sections is automatically tax-neutral.

UK tax treatment depends on individual circumstances and the type of transaction. For background reading, explore our overview of HMRC crypto tax reporting considerations; seek qualified advice for personal questions.

A practical checklist before choosing an app

First, identify whether the product is a share, fund, derivative, cryptoasset or other instrument. Second, read the separate terms for each product. Third, review GBP deposits and withdrawals. Fourth, compare the total cost shown at the point of transaction. Fifth, confirm whether asset transfers are supported. Finally, review risk disclosures and the relevant legal entity.

An app that lets you trade stocks and crypto UK products may be convenient, but convenience alone says little about suitability. You can use the same checklist for any provider without treating this guide as a recommendation.

Frequently asked questions

Can I hold stocks and cryptocurrency in one account?

Some interfaces support both, but the products may sit in different accounts or under different agreements. Check the legal and custody details.

Are stocks and crypto protected in the same way?

Not necessarily. Protection depends on the product, service and eligibility; losses from market movements are a separate risk.

Can I withdraw cryptocurrency to my own wallet?

Some services support external transfers and others do not. Confirm the supported networks and conditions in your account.

What is the best stock and crypto app UK readers should use?

There is no universally suitable app. Compare product type, ownership, costs, transfers and protections against your own needs.
