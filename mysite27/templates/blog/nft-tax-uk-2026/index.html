NFT Tax UK (2026/27): Buying, Selling and Creating NFTs

Copied

NFT tax UK questions depend on the role you play in a transaction. A collector selling a digital artwork, a creator minting work for customers and a trader buying NFTs repeatedly may face different UK tax outcomes. The label “NFT” does not, by itself, determine whether an amount is subject to Capital Gains Tax, Income Tax or business tax rules.

This guide explains NFT tax rules UK readers should consider in the 2026/27 tax year. It covers buying, selling, minting, creator royalties, record keeping and reporting. It is general education, not a determination of the tax treatment of any particular NFT or contract.

Quick answer: how are NFTs taxed in the UK?

How are NFTs taxed in the UK? For an individual holding an NFT as an investment, a sale or other disposal may produce a chargeable gain or allowable loss. For a creator operating a business, receipts from primary sales or royalties may instead be taxable income. The facts, contractual rights, frequency of activity and nature of the asset matter. Paying with cryptocurrency may create a separate cryptoasset disposal.

NFT tax UK: creator, buyer and seller scenarios

Creators: initial sales and NFT royalties

NFT tax for creators UK rules cannot be reduced to a single Capital Gains Tax percentage. Where an artist creates NFTs as part of a trade or professional activity, receipts from initial sales may form part of taxable business income. NFT tax for artists UK considerations include whether the creator is self-employed, what rights the buyer receives and which expenses are deductible under the relevant business rules.

NFT royalties tax UK treatment also depends on the underlying rights and business circumstances. A payment described as a royalty on a marketplace does not automatically qualify for a particular tax category. Keep records of the contract, payment date, GBP value and any platform deductions. VAT may also require separate consideration for a business; the NFT label alone does not settle VAT treatment.

Buyers: acquiring an NFT with GBP or crypto

NFT tax on buying UK collectors should distinguish the acquisition of an NFT from the use of cryptocurrency to pay for it. Buying an NFT with GBP does not normally create a capital gain merely because you acquired it. But spending Ether or another token to purchase the NFT may itself be a disposal of that cryptocurrency, potentially generating a separate gain or loss.

Record the NFT token identifier, purchase date, wallet address, consideration paid in GBP and any eligible acquisition expenses. Where payment was made in crypto, record the GBP market value of the crypto disposed of at that time and preserve its acquisition history. NFT minting tax UK issues may arise when a buyer mints from a project contract rather than purchasing on a secondary marketplace.

Sellers: resales and disposals

Tax on selling NFTs UK investors should consider the proceeds of the sale, allowable acquisition cost and any deductible disposal expenses. For a personal investment, NFT capital gains tax UK treatment may apply. For someone whose activity amounts to trading, profits may instead fall within Income Tax or business rules. Selling frequently does not, by itself, prove a trade; the overall facts matter.

NFT tax on selling UK rules can also apply to transfers for something other than cash, including exchanges for another NFT or cryptoasset. A gift may involve market-value rules depending on the recipient and circumstances. A transfer between wallets under the same beneficial ownership is different from a disposal, although records should still explain it.

Worked GBP example: NFT sale and separate crypto payment

Assume a UK collector acquired an NFT for £1,200 and later sold it for £2,000. Suppose £80 of costs qualify as allowable acquisition or disposal expenses. On those simplified assumptions, the potential capital gain on the NFT is £720 (£2,000 − £1,200 − £80). The actual result depends on the asset, rights, ownership, expenses and the person’s overall tax position.

Now assume the collector originally paid for the NFT using Ether worth £1,200 on the purchase date. If that Ether had a correctly calculated allowable cost of £900, spending it could also have created a separate £300 cryptoasset gain at the time of purchase. The later NFT gain and the earlier Ether disposal are distinct events; neither is automatically ignored because the transactions occurred on the same platform.

For 2026/27, the individual Capital Gains Tax annual exempt amount is £3,000 across relevant net chargeable gains, not £3,000 per NFT. The standard individual CGT rates are 18% and 24% depending on circumstances. These rates and allowance are not a substitute for checking whether a particular creator’s receipts are income rather than capital gains.

How to work out NFT capital gains tax UK calculations

For a capital transaction, start with the GBP value of disposal proceeds, subtract the relevant allowable acquisition cost and any legally allowable incidental costs, then consider applicable losses and reliefs. Do not use the current floor price of a collection as the value of a historic transaction. Retain evidence of the price actually paid or received and any relevant market-value rules.

Unlike fungible cryptoassets, individual NFTs are often distinguishable by contract and token identifier. Do not assume the section 104 pooling approach used for identical fungible tokens applies mechanically to every NFT. The legal and factual characteristics of the asset determine the correct treatment.

Can NFT losses UK investors make be claimed?

NFT losses UK collectors experience may be allowable capital losses where a qualifying disposal has occurred and the tax rules permit the claim. A fall in an NFT’s listed price is not automatically a realised loss. Theft, scams, lost access and worthless assets require their own factual analysis; a negligible-value claim is not available merely because a marketplace displays a low bid.

NFT tax reporting UK: records and deadlines

HMRC NFT tax enquiries are easier to answer when the transaction history is complete. Keep acquisition and disposal dates, token identifiers, smart-contract addresses, wallet addresses, marketplace receipts, GBP valuations, crypto payments, gas fees, royalty receipts and the basis for classifying income versus gains.

For NFT tax reporting UK, the relevant Self Assessment and Capital Gains Tax reporting obligations depend on your overall circumstances. For the 2026/27 tax year ending 5 April 2027, the usual online Self Assessment filing and payment deadline is 31 January 2028, where Self Assessment applies. Registration and other notification requirements may have earlier deadlines. Check the latest HMRC guidance before filing.

How NFT tax differs from ordinary crypto tax

NFT trading tax UK searches often mix up creator receipts, investment gains and payment-token disposals. An NFT may represent artwork, a membership right, a gaming asset or a contractual claim; that underlying substance can affect tax treatment. HMRC guidance on cryptoassets provides a starting point, but complex rights, VAT, business receipts and cross-border arrangements may need individual advice.

Frequently asked questions about NFT tax UK

Do I pay tax when I mint an NFT?

Minting an NFT does not establish one universal tax result. The circumstances of creation, any payment received, business activity, and cryptocurrency used for minting fees can matter. NFT minting tax UK treatment should be considered alongside the creator’s wider activity.

Is every NFT resale subject to Capital Gains Tax?

No. A personal investment resale may be within Capital Gains Tax, but profits from trading or a business may be income. Some disposals may produce no chargeable gain after the applicable calculation and allowances.

Do NFT creators pay Income Tax on royalties?

NFT income tax UK questions about royalties depend on whether the receipts are business income, intellectual-property income or another category under the facts. The marketplace label is not decisive.

Can an NFT loss offset a Bitcoin gain?

An allowable capital loss from an NFT may generally be considered against chargeable capital gains, subject to the applicable rules. It cannot automatically be set against salary or creator business income.

Is the NFT tax allowance UK different from the normal CGT allowance?

For the nft tax allowance uk question, there is no general separate annual CGT allowance for each NFT. The individual annual exempt amount applies across relevant chargeable gains, subject to the tax rules.
