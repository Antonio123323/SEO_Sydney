Best Crypto Exchanges in the UK (2026): Fees, GBP Funding & FCA Status

Copied

There is no single best crypto exchange UK choice for every user. The right platform depends on how you fund the account, whether you use a simple buy screen or an order book, how often you trade, whether you withdraw to your own wallet, and which legal entity actually serves UK customers.

This guide compares six current UK-facing options using the same practical checks: FCA status, GBP funding, cost structure, trading interface, withdrawal flexibility and the type of user each platform suits. We do not treat FCA registration as an endorsement, and we do not call a platform “safe” simply because it appears on an FCA register.

Risk note: Cryptoassets are high-risk and prices can fall sharply. You could lose all the money you invest. Direct cryptoasset services are generally not covered by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme, and FCA registration under the Money Laundering Regulations is not an FCA recommendation or approval.

Quick answer: which UK crypto exchange should you compare first?

If you want the best crypto exchange for a straightforward UK journey, start by comparing the platform’s GBP route, total cost and withdrawal options rather than choosing by headline trading fee alone. For an exchange-style experience, Kraken is a strong all-round reference point; Coinbase combines a simple interface with a more advanced trading route; Crypto.com is app-first; Bitpanda is broker-style; eToro suits people who also use traditional investments; and Revolut X can be convenient for existing Revolut users.

For many readers, the best UK crypto exchange will therefore be the one that fits their actual workflow rather than the one with the longest coin list. The comparison below is a use-case shortlist, not a guarantee that any platform will remain available on identical terms after publication.

What FCA registration means in 2026 — and what it does not mean

Searches for FCA approved crypto exchanges are common, but “FCA approved” is not the right description for ordinary spot-crypto platforms today. Under the current Money Laundering Regulations, in-scope UK cryptoasset businesses must register with the FCA for AML/CTF supervision. The FCA explicitly says that this registration is a legal requirement, not a recommendation or endorsement.

That distinction matters when comparing regulated crypto exchanges UK users can access. A company may also hold separate FCA permissions for electronic money, investment services or another regulated activity, but those permissions do not automatically turn every crypto service it offers into an FCA-protected product.

The UK is moving to a broader FSMA crypto regime. The FCA says the new regime is expected to commence on 25 October 2027, when firms carrying on newly regulated cryptoasset activities will generally need the relevant FCA authorisation unless an exemption or transition applies. Until then, always read the legal entity and the specific permission, not just the brand name.

How we compared the platforms

Rather than declaring the top crypto exchanges UK users should choose based on a single fee number, we used six checks that are repeatable when you compare any platform:

  • UK availability: can a UK retail user actually access the relevant service now?
  • Legal entity and status: which company serves the user, and what does its FCA registration or authorisation cover?
  • GBP funding: is there a practical sterling route, and are bank-transfer requirements clear?
  • Total cost: trading fee, spread or quote markup, payment fee, currency conversion and withdrawal/network cost.
  • Trading route: simple buy screen, broker quote, order book or advanced interface.
  • Custody and exit: whether supported assets can be withdrawn to a personal wallet and what the platform charges or restricts.

This is also why a search for the best crypto trading platform UK can return both exchanges and multi-asset brokers. Those products can solve similar user problems, but their pricing and custody models are not identical, so compare like with like.

1. Kraken — strong all-round exchange-style option

Kraken is useful for UK users who want both a straightforward purchase flow and a more traditional order-book interface. Payward Ltd is registered with the FCA as a cryptoasset firm under the MLRs (FRN 928768), while Payward Services Limited is FCA-authorised as an Electronic Money Institution (FRN 1010381).

The key cost lesson is to compare Kraken’s simple purchase route with Kraken Pro. Kraken’s current official fee schedule states that Instant Buy/Sell carries a trading fee plus a spread, while Kraken Pro uses maker/taker tiers. The exact rate can change with volume, product and account status, so use the live preview before confirming an order.

2. Coinbase — beginner-friendly flow with an advanced route

Coinbase works well for users who want a familiar beginner interface but may later want a more advanced trading screen. Its UK agreement says CB Payments Ltd is registered under the MLRs and authorised as an Electronic Money Institution under FRN 900635; it also holds separate MiFID investment-firm authorisation under FRN 1045733. Those permissions should still be read service by service.

UK customers can fund a GBP balance using Faster Payments or Easy Bank Transfer, subject to verification and account rules. As with other platforms, compare the final quote in the simple-buy flow with the cost of using an advanced trading interface before deciding which route is cheaper for your order size.

3. Crypto.com — app-first experience and broad choice

Crypto.com is primarily an app-first choice for UK users who value a broad range of cryptoassets and an integrated consumer experience. Foris DAX UK Limited is registered with the FCA under the MLRs to provide cryptoasset services (FRN 941745). Crypto.com also makes clear that other products in its app may be provided by other group companies, so check which entity sits behind the feature you are using.

For GBP users, the important comparison is the all-in quote rather than a single “trading fee” label. Check the amount of crypto received for a fixed GBP spend, then compare any later withdrawal cost if you intend to move assets to your own wallet.

4. Bitpanda — broker-style simplicity

Bitpanda Broker UK Ltd is registered with the FCA under the MLRs (FRN 925234). Its model is closer to a broker-style quote than a classic exchange order book, which can be easier for beginners but means you should compare the displayed buy price, sell price and any withdrawal cost rather than looking only for maker/taker fees.

This can be a good fit for a user who wants a clean purchase journey and does not need advanced order types. It is less suitable if your priority is granular order-book control or very active trading.

5. eToro — multi-asset users who also buy crypto

eToro is not a pure crypto exchange: it is a multi-asset platform. eToro (UK) Ltd is FCA-authorised under FRN 583263 and is also registered to offer cryptocurrency services under the MLRs. That makes it relevant for users who want stocks and other investments alongside crypto, but it should not be compared with an exchange order book as if the products were identical.

Before using it for crypto, check the current trade cost, whether the asset can be transferred or withdrawn in the way you expect, and whether the feature you want is available to UK retail clients.

6. Revolut X — convenient for users already in the Revolut ecosystem

Revolut Ltd is registered with the FCA to offer cryptoasset services under the MLRs (FRN 900562). Revolut X gives users an exchange-style interface, while the main Revolut app has its own crypto pricing structure and minimum fees. The practical lesson is to check which interface you are using before comparing costs.

For an existing Revolut user, that integration can reduce friction. For someone choosing a platform purely for crypto, compare the quoted trading cost and external withdrawal cost against standalone exchanges before deciding.

Fees: why “low commission” is not the same as low total cost

If your goal is the cheapest crypto exchange UK option, compare the whole transaction from pounds in your bank account to crypto in the destination you actually want. A platform can advertise a low trading fee while still producing a higher total cost through a wider spread, card charge, conversion, or withdrawal fee.

Likewise, a search for the best crypto exchange UK low fees should be split into two scenarios: a simple one-off purchase and an advanced order-book trade. The cheaper interface for one scenario may not be the cheaper interface for the other.

GBP funding: the bank route can matter as much as the exchange

A crypto exchange in UK search results may support GBP, but your own bank can still delay, block or query crypto-related payments. Use the payment details shown inside your verified account, send from an account in your own name where required, and never reuse old bank details from screenshots or past transfers.

If your bank repeatedly blocks legitimate payments, compare crypto-friendly banks in the UK before changing payment methods. A bank-transfer issue does not automatically mean the exchange itself is unavailable.

For a UK cryptocurrency exchange, Faster Payments or another direct GBP route can simplify the journey because it reduces avoidable currency conversion. But availability, limits and timing remain account-specific, so the live deposit screen is the final source of truth.

Custody and withdrawals: decide where the crypto should end up

The platform you use to buy crypto does not have to be the place where you hold it long term. Some users prefer exchange custody for convenience; others withdraw supported assets to a personal wallet. Neither choice removes risk.

If self-custody is part of your plan, choose a crypto wallet for UK users before sending a large amount. Check the network, destination address, minimum withdrawal and total fee first, and test with a small transfer when appropriate.

How to choose the right platform for your use case

  1. Decide whether you want a simple buy flow, broker quote or order-book interface.
  2. Verify the UK legal entity and the exact FCA status. Do not treat MLR registration as an endorsement.
  3. Check that your preferred GBP funding method is available in your own account.
  4. Price the full journey: funding, execution, spread, conversion and withdrawal.
  5. Confirm that the coins, order types and withdrawal networks you need are actually available to UK retail users.
  6. Use strong account security: unique password, app-based 2FA where available, and withdrawal/address controls.
  7. Recheck the platform’s live fee and legal pages before a large transaction because both product access and pricing can change.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best crypto exchange in the UK?

The best choice depends on your workflow. Kraken is a useful benchmark for an exchange-style experience, Coinbase for a beginner-to-advanced path, Crypto.com for an app-first experience, Bitpanda for broker-style simplicity, eToro for multi-asset users and Revolut X for people already using Revolut. Compare live fees and availability before choosing.

Which crypto exchanges are regulated in UK?

The phrase which crypto exchanges are regulated in UK needs careful wording. In 2026, many crypto firms serving UK users are registered with the FCA under the Money Laundering Regulations for AML/CTF supervision. Some brands also have separate authorised entities for electronic money or investment services. The broader crypto FSMA regime is expected to commence on 25 October 2027.

Are there FCA approved crypto exchanges?

“FCA approved” is not the right shorthand for ordinary spot-crypto services. The FCA states that MLR registration is a legal requirement and is not a recommendation or endorsement. Check the entity, FRN and permission on the FCA register and the platform’s own legal page.

What is the cheapest crypto exchange for UK users?

There is no permanent cheapest option because the result changes with order size, interface, payment method, spread, trading volume and withdrawal plan. Compare the same GBP scenario across two or three platforms using the final confirmation screen, not just the advertised commission rate.

What is the best crypto app UK users can use?

The best crypto app UK users can choose depends on whether they prioritise a simple app, advanced trading, multi-asset investing or integration with an existing banking app. App convenience should still be checked against total cost, withdrawal options and UK product restrictions.

Do UK crypto exchanges require ID checks?

Legitimate UK-facing platforms generally apply identity and anti-money-laundering checks. Requirements vary by platform and account. Avoid treating “no KYC” as a shortcut around compliance or geographic restrictions.

Is money on a crypto exchange protected by FSCS?

Do not assume so. Direct cryptoasset services are generally outside normal FSCS protection, and the FCA warns consumers not to expect compensation if a crypto investment or unregulated service goes wrong. Separate regulated payment or investment services offered by the same group can have different protections, so read the exact terms for the service you use.

Final checklist before you open or fund an account

  • The platform currently accepts UK retail users for the service you want.
  • You have checked the exact UK legal entity and FCA register status.
  • GBP funding is available to your account and your bank is compatible with the payment route.
  • You understand the total purchase cost, not only the headline trading fee.
  • You know whether and how you can withdraw the asset to your own wallet.
  • You have enabled appropriate account security and will verify addresses carefully.
  • You have read the current risk warning and are prepared for the possibility of losing the money invested.
