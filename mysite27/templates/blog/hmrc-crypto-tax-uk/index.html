Crypto Tax UK in 2026: HMRC Rules, Gains and Tax Returns

Copied

Crypto tax UK rules can apply even when no money reaches your bank account. Selling tokens, exchanging one cryptoasset for another or spending crypto can create a taxable disposal. Receiving tokens from employment, staking or other activities can instead raise Income Tax questions. This guide explains HMRC crypto tax rules for individual UK taxpayers, with examples, record-keeping steps and dates relevant to the 2025/26 and 2026/27 tax years. It is educational information, not personalised tax advice.

Quick answer: do you pay tax on crypto in the UK?

Yes, potentially. Buying and simply holding crypto generally does not itself create Capital Gains Tax. However, crypto capital gains tax may arise when you sell, swap, spend or give away tokens, subject to the applicable exemptions. Tokens received as earnings or certain rewards may count as income. Whether tax is payable depends on the full tax-year position, allowable costs, losses and personal circumstances. Do not assume that converting Bitcoin into another token is tax-free.

Understand HMRC Crypto Tracking

Which crypto transactions are taxable?

A practical crypto tax rules UK checklist starts with classifying each transaction. The following distinctions are a guide, not a substitute for HMRC's detailed treatment of unusual arrangements.

  • Buying crypto with GBP and continuing to hold it: generally no disposal at purchase; keep acquisition and fee records.
  • Selling crypto for GBP: normally a disposal for Capital Gains Tax purposes.
  • Swapping Bitcoin for Ether or another token: normally a disposal of the token given up, even without a bank withdrawal.
  • Using tokens to pay for goods or services: normally a disposal.
  • Giving tokens to another person: can be a disposal; special rules can apply to transfers to a spouse, civil partner or charity.
  • Moving tokens between wallets you beneficially own: generally not a disposal by itself, although network fees and complex arrangements need separate review.
  • Receiving employment income, mining or staking rewards: may trigger Income Tax and sometimes National Insurance considerations, depending on the facts.

UK tax years, allowances and crypto tax rates

A UK tax year runs from 6 April to 5 April, not from January to December. The 2025/26 tax year ended on 5 April 2026; the 2026/27 year runs from 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027. The annual exempt amount for most individuals is £3,000 in both years. That is an allowance against qualifying net capital gains, not a blanket £3,000 exemption for every crypto transaction or for staking income.

For ordinary individual gains, the crypto tax rate depends on income: the Capital Gains Tax rates applicable in both 2025/26 and 2026/27 are 18% and 24%. The relevant rate depends on taxable income and the amount of gains; some special situations differ. To answer 'how much tax on crypto gains', first calculate all chargeable gains and losses for the tax year, apply the correct annual exempt amount and then determine which part falls within each rate band. Income Tax on crypto rewards follows different rules.

How to calculate crypto tax: GBP values and allowable costs

To understand capital gains tax on crypto UK residents normally need a GBP valuation at each relevant event. Start with disposal proceeds, deduct the allowable acquisition cost determined under the identification rules and account for qualifying transaction fees. The result is a gain or loss for that disposal. Reconcile exchange exports, wallet transactions and fiat payment records: an exchange's downloadable report is not necessarily a complete UK tax calculation.

Allowable costs can include acquisition cost and certain transaction fees, subject to HMRC conditions. Costs already deducted for Income Tax cannot normally be deducted again. Maintain evidence of the GBP rate used, the timestamp, token quantity and fees. An unexplained spreadsheet total is less useful than a traceable transaction history.

Same-day matching, the 30-day rule and Section 104 pooling

HMRC crypto capital gains tax calculations use identification rules rather than a simple first-in, first-out method. Broadly, disposals are matched first against acquisitions of the same token on the same day, then against acquisitions of the same token in the following 30 days, with the remaining amount matched to the Section 104 pool. The pool records the aggregate quantity and allowable cost for each token type. These rules matter when someone sells and soon buys back the same token.

Illustrative pooled-cost example, assuming no same-day or 30-day matching: buy 2 units of Token A for £1,000 total, then 3 more for £2,100 total. The pool contains 5 units costing £3,100, or £620 per unit. Sell 2 units for £1,600, with no deductible selling fees. Matched cost is £1,240 and the illustrative gain is £360. The remaining pool holds 3 units with £1,860 cost. This is an arithmetic example, not a tax bill.

For a separate 30-day example, suppose a person sells one unit of Token B on 10 June and acquires one unit of the same token on 25 June. The later acquisition falls within 30 days, so the normal pooled average cannot simply be used for that sale. The correct matching order and any same-day transactions must be checked before calculating the gain.

Staking, mining and crypto income tax UK

Crypto income tax UK questions require a separate analysis: not all crypto activity is taxed as a capital gain. HMRC guidance states that cryptoassets received through employment and activities such as mining, staking or lending may be taxable as income. Treatment depends on whether activities amount to a trade and on the arrangement. Where Income Tax applies to the value received, a later disposal can create a separate gain or loss measured against the appropriate acquisition value; the same value should not be taxed twice as income and as the initial capital gain.

For a dedicated explanation of reward scenarios, read our guide to UK staking rewards and tax treatment. Record the date, token amount and GBP market value when a reward is received, as well as any later disposal. A 'crypto staking tax UK' question cannot be answered reliably from reward percentages alone.

Losses, gifts and special cases

Capital losses on chargeable disposals can sometimes reduce taxable gains, provided they are calculated and reported under the relevant rules. Losses are not the same as a drop in the quoted price of unsold tokens. Gifts, transactions between connected persons, negligible-value claims and lost access to wallets can involve additional conditions; do not automatically treat a stolen or inaccessible token as a deductible disposal.

Keep the tax treatment of decentralised finance, liquidity pools, airdrops and NFTs separate where the facts differ. For complicated transactions, use HMRC's Cryptoassets Manual or obtain advice from a qualified UK tax professional rather than copying a generic online crypto tax rate.

How to report crypto tax to HMRC

A crypto tax return may be needed when chargeable gains, other income or HMRC reporting requirements make Self Assessment necessary. When preparing a crypto tax return, remember that the Self Assessment return has a dedicated cryptoasset section for tax years from 2024/25 onwards. HMRC also describes a Capital Gains Tax real-time reporting service for relevant gains; check which route fits your circumstances. Reporting thresholds and obligations are not identical to the question of whether a gain is ultimately taxed.

For the 2025/26 tax year, the usual deadline to notify HMRC about a new Self Assessment obligation was 5 October 2026. The online return and balancing payment are normally due by 31 January 2027. If registration was late, HMRC may give a different return-filing date, but that does not automatically move the tax-payment deadline. For 2026/27, the usual corresponding dates are 5 October 2027 and 31 January 2028, subject to any subsequent changes. Always confirm the deadlines applicable to your case on GOV.UK.

If you are unsure whether HMRC already has transaction information, our separate article on how HMRC receives crypto transaction data explains reporting and exchange records. This article instead focuses on calculating and declaring the taxpayer's own position.

What crypto records should you keep?

Save complete exchange exports, wallet addresses, transaction IDs, token quantities, GBP values, fees, acquisition and disposal dates, income-receipt details, bank statements and the calculations used for same-day, 30-day and pooled matching. Reconcile transfers between accounts so a movement between your own wallets is not accidentally recorded as a sale. Our guide to crypto wallet records and custody explains wallet terminology that can help when building this audit trail.

Keep records even when the annual exempt amount appears to cover a gain. HMRC may request evidence, and a later transaction can change how earlier acquisitions are matched. A commercial crypto tax calculator can assist with reconciliation, but it is not an official HMRC crypto tax calculator and does not remove your responsibility to review its outputs.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using calendar-year totals rather than the 6 April–5 April UK tax year.
  • Treating token-to-token swaps as non-taxable merely because no GBP was withdrawn.
  • Applying the capital gains annual exempt amount to taxable staking income.
  • Ignoring the same-day and 30-day identification rules.
  • Relying on one exchange export while overlooking other wallets and platforms.
  • Confusing platform reporting under CARF with the taxpayer’s own filing deadline.

Crypto tax UK FAQs

Is crypto taxable if I only hold it?

Buying and holding tokens usually does not itself create a taxable disposal. Tax may arise when tokens are disposed of or received as taxable income.

Do you pay tax on crypto when swapping tokens?

A token-to-token exchange is normally a disposal of the token given up. Work out its GBP value and applicable acquisition cost.

How much tax do you pay on crypto gains?

It depends on net gains, reliefs, other taxable income and the relevant tax year. The 18% and 24% CGT rates are not a single flat charge on the total amount withdrawn.

Is there an official HMRC crypto tax calculator?

HMRC provides guidance and tax reporting services, but do not assume a third-party calculator is endorsed by HMRC. Check its transaction matching and GBP figures against HMRC rules.

When do I pay tax on crypto UK gains?

For a Self Assessment liability relating to 2025/26, the usual payment deadline is 31 January 2027. Other reporting methods or special circumstances can have different requirements.
