How to Send GBP to Binance from a UK Bank in 2026

Copied

If you already have a UK Binance account and your account currently shows a GBP bank-transfer option, you may be able to fund it from a UK bank using the payment route displayed inside Binance. The important part in 2026 is not to follow an old screenshot or a saved payee blindly: GBP availability, payment partners, fees and limits can change, and the details shown in your own verified account are the source of truth.

Binance’s UK-specific update says it stopped accepting new UK users from 16 October 2023. This guide is therefore written for existing UK users who can already access the relevant service. If GBP bank transfer is missing from your account, do not try to bypass regional or banking restrictions. Use the alternatives section below instead.

Before you send money: Only transfer to bank details generated inside your current Binance session. Do not use recipient details copied from an old article, message, email or saved payee without checking them again. Cryptoasset services are high risk and normal UK protections may not apply.

Quick answer: how to deposit GBP to Binance from the UK

  1. Sign in through the official Binance app or website and confirm that your existing account is verified.
  2. Open Deposit / Fiat Deposit, choose GBP, and select Bank Transfer or Faster Payments if that option is shown to you.
  3. Review the current fee, limits, recipient bank details and any unique payment reference before leaving the Binance screen.
  4. Send the transfer from a personal UK bank account in the same name as your Binance account. Copy the payee details and reference exactly.
  5. Save the bank receipt and check Binance transaction history. Do not immediately resend the payment if the first transfer is still processing.

Can UK users still make GBP deposits to Binance in 2026?

Binance’s current support centre still includes a GBP Transfer category, and its Faster Payments troubleshooting page was updated in March 2026. Binance also has a live GBP fiat-deposit page. That means GBP funding routes remain documented for supported accounts, but it does not mean every UK user will see the same method at the same time.

The safest rule is simple: if GBP bank transfer is not visible after you log in, treat it as unavailable to your account. Do not send money to bank details from a previous deposit, and do not switch residence details or use somebody else’s account to force access.

Before the transfer: five checks that prevent most problems

  1. Your Binance account is verified. Binance states that identity verification is required to access products and services, and GBP transfer limits can depend on verification status.
  2. The sender name matches. Binance’s GBP Faster Payments FAQ says the bank-account name should match the name registered on the Binance account.
  3. You are using a personal account. The current GBP FPS FAQ says the channel supports personal accounts and warns that joint-account transfers may be declined.
  4. Your bank permits the payment. UK banks can apply their own controls to crypto-related transfers. A bank decline does not necessarily mean the Binance details are wrong.
  5. The payment method is the one shown in your account. Do not substitute SWIFT for a Faster Payments route or assume a card/PayPal method is available because an old guide mentioned it.

How to send GBP to Binance by bank transfer: step by step

1. Open the GBP deposit route inside Binance

Log in through the official app or website. Open the deposit area, choose fiat currency, select GBP and then choose the bank-transfer route that is actually available to your account. The interface can change, so follow the labels shown in the current session rather than matching an old tutorial screen-for-screen.

2. Read the fee, limit and timing before continuing

Binance’s GBP Faster Payments FAQ, updated 5 March 2026, lists a £1 deposit fee and a £1 withdrawal fee for FPS and says processing can take from a few minutes up to one business day depending on the bank. The same page explicitly says this information may change, so the figure on your live deposit screen wins if it is different.

3. Copy the recipient details and payment reference

Copy the recipient name, sort code/account details and any reference shown by Binance. Do not improvise a reference and do not reuse a reference from another person. If Binance presents different details from a previous transfer, use the current details.

4. Send the money from your own matching bank account

Open your bank app or online banking and create the payment using the details from the Binance screen. The sender name should match the verified Binance account. Binance’s current FPS help page warns that joint-account transfers are likely to be declined because multiple names may not match the Binance account name.

5. Confirm the amount and authorise the bank payment

Review the amount, payee and reference one final time before authorising. If your bank shows a crypto or investment warning, read it rather than clicking through automatically. Never follow instructions from someone contacting you by phone, Telegram, WhatsApp or social media to “help” with the transfer.

6. Check Binance transaction history instead of resending immediately

After the bank confirms the payment, return to Binance and check the fiat wallet or transaction history. A transfer can be delayed by bank processing, identity checks or manual compliance review. Keep the bank receipt until the deposit is credited or returned.

Why is my Binance bank transfer pending or missing?

  • Identity verification is incomplete. Binance says pending verification can delay crediting. Finish any verification request shown in your account.
  • The bank-account name does not match. Transfers from a different person or a joint account can be rejected or returned.
  • You used the wrong rail. Binance’s GBP FPS FAQ says SWIFT is not the correct route for FPS. Use the method shown in your account.
  • The payment reference was wrong or omitted. Check the live deposit instructions and contact support with the bank receipt rather than sending a second payment.
  • You hit an account limit. Limits depend on verification level. Check the current deposit limit before splitting or repeating payments.
  • Your bank stopped the payment. Banks can block or review crypto-related transfers under their own policies. Do not disguise the purpose or alter payment information to get around a block.
  • The transfer is under manual review. Compliance review can add time even when the bank side looks complete. Keep evidence and use official support.

What if GBP bank transfer is not available in your Binance account?

Do not send money to old Binance bank details and do not use another person’s account. First, confirm that you are on the genuine Binance domain, that your country of residence is correct and that there is no verification request waiting in the account. If the GBP route still does not appear, use a UK-available alternative rather than trying to work around the restriction.

Bank transfer vs card: which funding method is better?

A bank transfer and a card payment solve the same basic problem—moving pounds into a crypto service—but the cost and bank controls can differ. A bank transfer often has a small fixed platform fee when supported, while card routes may have a higher percentage cost or additional issuer checks. Do not assume the card is cheaper or faster. Compare the exact fee and the amount you will receive on the confirmation screen.

  • Use bank transfer when the route is supported, the fee is clear and your bank permits the payment.
  • Use a card only if the current Binance screen offers it to your account and you are comfortable with the displayed cost.
  • Do not choose a payment method because a third-party guide says it is available; check your own account first.

Security checks before every GBP transfer

  • Open Binance from a saved official bookmark or type the address yourself; avoid deposit links sent in DMs or ads.
  • Use two-factor authentication and never share a one-time code with someone claiming to be support.
  • Generate recipient details inside the current deposit flow each time instead of relying on old screenshots.
  • Check the payee, sort code/account number, reference and amount before authorising the bank payment.
  • If a caller tells you to move money to “protect” it or to unlock an account, stop and contact the bank and platform through official channels.

UK access and regulation: what this guide does—and does not—mean

A working GBP deposit route is not the same thing as FCA authorisation or consumer protection. Binance’s UK-facing pages include risk warnings, and its UK update states that new UK user onboarding was stopped from 16 October 2023 while existing users retained the services then available subject to the relevant UK investor steps. Access can therefore be different for an existing user and someone trying to open a new account.

The FCA’s crypto financial-promotion rules apply to firms marketing qualifying cryptoassets to UK consumers, but that does not make the underlying cryptoasset protected by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme. Treat this article as an operational guide for a payment route, not as a recommendation to use Binance or to buy a particular cryptoasset.
