UK Crypto Regulation in 2026: Current FCA Rules and the 2027 Regime

Copied

UK crypto regulation is changing, but not every announced rule applies today. In November 2026, the United Kingdom already has requirements covering certain cryptoasset businesses, anti-money laundering supervision and financial promotions. A wider Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) authorisation regime is scheduled to begin on 25 October 2027. Understanding that timeline is essential when evaluating a crypto service.

This guide explains cryptocurrency regulation UK readers encounter now, what the new UK crypto regulations mean for businesses and what consumers can check before transferring money or digital assets. It separates rules already in force from future requirements and does not treat FCA registration as an investment endorsement.

Quick answer: is cryptocurrency regulated in the UK in 2026?

Yes, but the scope matters. Under current UK crypto rules, relevant cryptoasset exchange providers and custodian wallet providers may need FCA registration under the Money Laundering Regulations. The UK financial promotions regime also governs many promotions of qualifying cryptoassets to consumers. However, FCA crypto registration under the anti-money laundering framework is not the same as full FSMA authorisation for a crypto trading or custody service. The wider cryptoasset regulation UK regime is due to start on 25 October 2027.

UK crypto regulation timeline: what applies now and what comes next

Before 2026: registration and financial promotions

FCA crypto regulation already covers specific business activities. The Money Laundering Regulations require certain in-scope cryptoasset firms operating in the UK to register with the FCA for anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing supervision. Separate rules on cryptoasset financial promotions, introduced in 2023, apply to many communications directed at UK consumers. These regimes have different purposes, tests and legal consequences.

2026: legislation made and applications open

The Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026 were made in February 2026. The FCA published final rules and guidance in June 2026 and further perimeter guidance in September. The application window for relevant cryptoasset permissions opened on 30 September 2026 and is scheduled to close on 28 February 2027. An application is not itself an authorisation.

25 October 2027: wider regime begins

The new UK crypto regulatory framework is expected to take effect on 25 October 2027. In-scope firms carrying out specified regulated cryptoasset activities will generally need appropriate FSMA authorisation or a variation of existing permissions. Businesses should assess their activities against FCA perimeter guidance; existing registration under the Money Laundering Regulations does not automatically become FSMA permission.

FCA crypto rules: registration versus authorisation

FCA crypto registration is primarily about supervision for financial crime controls under the existing Money Laundering Regulations. It does not mean the regulator has approved an exchange’s investments, token listings, solvency or future performance. Nor does it automatically give a consumer access to the Financial Services Compensation Scheme for crypto losses.

FCA authorisation under FSMA is a separate regulatory status tied to specified regulated activities and permissions. The forthcoming regime is designed to cover a broader range of cryptoasset services. Always inspect the exact legal entity, permissions, registration details and warnings on the official FCA Register rather than relying on a badge or marketing claim.

Which cryptoasset activities may be regulated?

The cryptoasset regulation UK perimeter depends on the nature of the asset, activity and firm. The forthcoming framework includes specified activities involving qualifying cryptoassets, such as operating trading platforms, dealing and arranging deals, safeguarding cryptoassets and arranging cryptoasset staking. Issuing qualifying stablecoins is also within the new framework. Not every NFT, token, decentralised protocol or overseas service is automatically covered in the same way.

For crypto trading regulation UK firms need to consider the particular activity they perform, not just whether they describe themselves as an exchange, broker, app or wallet. A custody provider holding customer assets raises different questions from a non-custodial software wallet. Marketing to UK consumers can also create obligations independently of whether the underlying business is UK-incorporated.

Financial promotions: what UK consumers should notice

The UK crypto regulations 2026 landscape includes existing financial promotion restrictions. Many promotions of qualifying cryptoassets must be communicated or approved through a lawful route. Depending on the promotion and route used, consumers may encounter prominent risk warnings, restrictions on incentives to invest and other safeguards. A professional-looking advert is not proof of regulatory compliance.

Crypto law UK rules on advertising should not be confused with a promise of compensation. Even where a promotion is lawful, cryptoassets can be volatile and users may lose their entire investment. A platform may also restrict withdrawals, stop supporting a network or experience operational problems. Read the actual risk disclosures and identify the firm responsible for the service.

Practical checks before using a crypto platform

1. Verify the exact business and permissions

Search the FCA Register for the firm’s legal name and compare its website, company details and permissions. Check whether a firm is registered for anti-money laundering purposes, authorised for particular regulated activities, or neither. Look for warnings about clones and unauthorised businesses. Do not assume that a similarly named company is the one you are dealing with.

2. Understand custody and withdrawals

Ask who controls the private keys, how withdrawals are approved, whether the firm can freeze an account and what happens if it becomes insolvent. A custodial wallet, self-custody wallet and bank account involve different legal relationships. Review supported networks, recovery options and the consequences of sending tokens to the wrong address.

3. Check payment and promotion claims

Confirm the name of the payment recipient, the bank transfer reference, applicable limits and the source of any advertised return. Be cautious about claims of FCA approval, guaranteed income or urgent deposits. To understand how payment restrictions differ from regulatory status, read our guide to crypto-friendly banks and payment checks in the UK.

4. Keep evidence and monitor changes

Save terms of service, transaction confirmations, wallet addresses, relevant disclosures and support correspondence. Recheck a firm’s regulatory status when new services or permissions are introduced. A firm can be properly registered for one purpose while lacking permissions for another activity.

UK crypto compliance: what businesses should prepare for

For businesses, UK crypto compliance includes determining whether the firm falls within the current anti-money laundering registration perimeter and which activities will require FSMA permission from October 2027. Relevant considerations include governance, risk management, safeguarding arrangements, financial crime controls, customer communications, record keeping and prudential requirements under the applicable rules.

The FCA application period runs from 30 September 2026 to 28 February 2027. Firms should consult the FCA gateway instructions and perimeter guidance for their business model. Filing an application does not confer permission to undertake a new regulated activity. Firms operating under the existing regime may face specific transitional arrangements, but those arrangements must not be assumed to apply universally.

Consumer protection: what regulation does not guarantee

A major misconception about crypto regulation 2026 UK searches is that regulation removes market risk. It does not. Whether the Financial Ombudsman Service or Financial Services Compensation Scheme is relevant depends on the specific activity, complaint and legal conditions. Neither is a blanket guarantee for cryptoasset holdings or trading losses. Check the official position for the product and firm in question.

Consumers should also distinguish a firm’s registration from the legality of each product it offers. The new regime may increase oversight of specified activities, but the protections available will still depend on the rules, permissions and circumstances. Frequently asked questions about UK crypto regulation

Is crypto legal in the UK?

Buying and holding cryptoassets is not generally prohibited, but UK cryptocurrency laws impose obligations on certain firms and activities. The legality of a particular service, promotion or transaction depends on the circumstances.

Does an FCA-registered crypto exchange have full authorisation?

Not necessarily. Registration under the Money Laundering Regulations is distinct from authorisation under FSMA. Check the precise status and permissions of the legal entity.

When do the new UK crypto regulations start?

The wider FSMA cryptoasset regime is expected to start on 25 October 2027. The relevant FCA application window opened on 30 September 2026 and closes on 28 February 2027 under the published timetable.

Does UK crypto regulation cover stablecoins and staking?

The forthcoming regime includes specified activities involving qualifying stablecoins and arranging cryptoasset staking, subject to its legal definitions and perimeter. It does not follow that every token or service has identical obligations.

Can I rely on an FCA logo shown on a crypto website?

No. Check the official FCA Register, the legal entity and the exact status. Fraudsters may imitate authorised or registered businesses.

Does FCA crypto regulation protect me from losses?

No blanket protection applies to crypto trading losses. Regulatory status and eligibility for any complaint or compensation scheme must be checked for the specific service.
