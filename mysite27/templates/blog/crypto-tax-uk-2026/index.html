UK Crypto Tax Update (2026): What Changed and What Still Applies

Copied

Quick answer: what changed in UK crypto tax?

The key UK crypto tax update is not a new tax on every wallet transfer. Under the crypto tax rules UK taxpayers need to distinguish taxable disposals from income-producing crypto activities, keep sterling records and apply the rules for their tax year. The Capital Gains Tax (CGT) annual exempt amount for individuals is £3,000 in both 2025/26 and 2026/27. Standard individual CGT rates are 18% and 24%, with the applicable rate depending on taxable income and gains. When searching for the crypto tax rate, check the tax year and type of activity rather than relying on outdated 10% and 20% figures.

2025/26 versus 2026/27: dates and rates

The UK tax year runs from 6 April to 5 April, not from January to December. The 2025/26 tax year covers 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026; 2026/27 covers 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027. Check the disposal date before applying an allowance or a reporting deadline. A transaction dated March 2026 belongs to 2025/26; a sale in May 2026 belongs to 2026/27.

For most individual crypto disposals in these years, the annual CGT exemption is £3,000 and the standard rates are 18% or 24%. The precise split may depend on how the gains sit alongside taxable income and other gains. The exemption applies to total chargeable gains, not separately to each token or exchange. Do not assume that a crypto portfolio has its own additional allowance.

The phrase crypto tax UK 2026 can refer to two different tax years: January to early April 2026 is still 2025/26, while the period from 6 April 2026 falls in 2026/27. The crypto tax allowance is not an allowance per exchange or per wallet. This matters when someone has gains from Bitcoin, other tokens, shares or other chargeable assets in the same tax year.

Crypto tax changes 2026: what is genuinely new?

For people looking for crypto tax changes 2026, the central distinction is between continuing CGT and Income Tax treatment and the newer CARF information-reporting framework. The 18% and 24% standard individual CGT rates did not first appear in April 2026, and the £3,000 exemption was already in force before 2025/26. The CARF reporting change is about information collection and exchange; it does not create a separate crypto tax rate.

Which crypto transactions can create a tax event?

Selling cryptocurrency for GBP can be a disposal for CGT. Exchanging Bitcoin for Ether can also be a disposal even when no fiat currency enters a bank account. Spending tokens on goods or services and giving crypto away, other than in certain exempt circumstances, may also trigger a disposal. By contrast, moving the same beneficially owned tokens between your own wallets is not normally a disposal merely because the address changes. Network fees and ownership facts still need documenting.

Receiving tokens from employment, mining or staking can raise Income Tax questions depending on the facts. If tokens are later disposed of, a separate CGT calculation may be needed, with care to avoid double-counting the value previously brought into income. Airdrops, DeFi arrangements and NFT transactions can have fact-specific outcomes. Avoid treating every incoming token as automatically tax-free or automatically taxable.

Crypto tax on staking UK, mining and token swaps

Crypto tax on staking UK receipts may involve Income Tax when tokens are received, depending on the arrangement and the facts. Crypto tax on mining UK activity can also depend on whether the activity is a trade or a non-trading activity. Subsequent disposals can create capital gains or losses. The crypto to crypto tax UK position is different from a mere wallet transfer: exchanging one token for another is generally a disposal, even when no GBP is withdrawn.

A simple GBP example: a disposal is not the same as the tax bill

Illustrative example: a person buys a token holding for £4,000 and later sells it for £6,500. Ignoring fees and matching rules for this illustration, the gain is £2,500. If this is the person’s only chargeable gain in 2026/27, and they can use the full £3,000 annual exempt amount, this example alone would not generate CGT. However, other gains, losses, costs, acquisitions and reporting conditions may change the result.

Now suppose the illustrative net gain is £5,000 rather than £2,500, and the full £3,000 exemption remains available. That leaves £2,000 of chargeable gains after the exemption. At 18% the illustrative CGT would be £360; at 24% it would be £480. This is not a prediction of an individual bill: the applicable rate may be split across bands and the acquisition cost may need recalculation under share-matching rules.

So how much tax on crypto gains might someone pay? The answer depends on their total gains, available reliefs and losses, annual exemption and taxable income. For a reader asking what is the tax on crypto gains, the percentage alone is not enough: the gain must first be calculated correctly in GBP, using relevant allowable costs and matching rules. The examples above are illustrations, not personal estimates.

Why pooling and matching still matter

HMRC does not generally let a person choose whichever historical purchase price gives the smallest gain. Identical tokens are normally considered under same-day matching, the 30-day rule and the Section 104 pool, as applicable. These rules can change the allowable cost of a disposal. For a detailed step-by-step calculation and reporting checklist, use the linked HMRC crypto tax guide rather than assuming a single average purchase price always applies.

For HMRC crypto capital gains tax, the Section 104 pool and same-day/30-day matching rules can be decisive. Capital gains tax on crypto UK disposals is based on the calculated chargeable gain, not simply the sale proceeds. The term crypto capital gains tax is often used broadly, but it should not be confused with Income Tax on certain crypto receipts.

What CARF changed from January 2026

A separate reporting development is the Cryptoasset Reporting Framework (CARF). UK reporting cryptoasset service providers began relevant due-diligence and record-keeping obligations on 1 January 2026. The first provider reports for calendar year 2026 are due by 31 May 2027. CARF reporting by a service provider is not the same thing as a personal Self Assessment tax return, and the CARF calendar year is not the UK personal tax year.

Platforms may request identifying information and tax-residence details. The scope of reportable users and transactions depends on the framework. If you want to understand the information-sharing side rather than calculate a tax bill, read our separate explanation of how HMRC receives cryptoasset data.

Records and deadlines: a practical update checklist

Keep a dated export of transactions from each exchange and wallet, including transfers, swaps, fees, staking receipts and relevant blockchain transaction IDs. Record fair market values in GBP at the relevant dates and retain supporting evidence of exchange rates and acquisition costs. Reconcile transfers between your own accounts so that an internal transfer is not accidentally counted as a sale. A platform tax summary may help, but it does not replace a review of missing wallets, unsupported networks or incorrectly classified transactions.

If you need Self Assessment, the normal online filing deadline is 31 January after the tax year ends, with registration obligations potentially arising earlier. For example, the usual online filing deadline for 2025/26 is 31 January 2027. Different circumstances can affect whether a return is required and when a tax liability must be paid. Check the official HMRC guidance for your own filing route.

Crypto tax reporting UK: returns, records and losses

Crypto tax reporting UK may involve Self Assessment or another applicable reporting route, depending on the taxpayer’s circumstances. A crypto tax return UK search should not be taken to mean that there is a separate universal crypto-only return. Keep crypto tax records that connect each disposal with acquisition dates, GBP values, fees and wallet movements. Crypto tax deadlines follow the relevant tax year and reporting route; a provider’s CARF filing deadline is not the individual’s tax-return deadline.

Crypto tax losses UK treatment is also evidence-led. A fall in the market price alone is not necessarily a realised capital loss; a disposal, an eligible negligible-value claim or another recognised event may be needed. Preserve records of acquisition costs and disposal proceeds and check claim rules before assuming losses can offset gains. For specific calculations and tax-filing steps, use the dedicated HMRC crypto tax article linked near the start.

Common mistakes in older UK crypto tax guides

A guide headed “2025 update” may still quote outdated 10%/20% CGT rates or suggest the £3,000 annual exempt amount first started in April 2025. The £3,000 amount already applied in 2024/25. Another common mistake is to describe CARF as an EU-only DAC8 measure: the UK CARF rules are a distinct domestic implementation of an OECD framework. Avoid assuming an exchange’s data report means every tax figure has already been calculated correctly.

Another misconception is that selling at a loss always eliminates a tax bill automatically. Loss recognition, evidence, claim deadlines and the same-day/30-day matching rules matter. Frequently asked questions

Is crypto taxable in the UK in 2026?

Potentially, yes. Tax treatment depends on the transaction and individual circumstances. A disposal may create a capital gain or loss; some receipts may be taxable as income.

Did the UK introduce a new crypto capital gains tax rate in 2026?

The standard individual CGT rates for 2026/27 are 18% and 24%, as in 2025/26. Do not use outdated 10% and 20% figures for these years.

Does exchanging one coin for another count?

A crypto-to-crypto exchange is generally a disposal for UK CGT purposes, even when proceeds are never converted into GBP.

Will CARF mean HMRC calculates my tax for me?

No. Reporting by service providers can give HMRC transaction information, but individuals remain responsible for checking their own records and tax obligations.

What is the crypto tax allowance in 2026/27?

The annual exempt amount for individual Capital Gains Tax in 2026/27 is £3,000. It applies across chargeable gains, not independently to each cryptoasset.

Do I need to report a transfer between my own wallets?

A transfer between wallets you beneficially own is not normally a disposal by itself, but keep records to prove the movement and reconcile fees and addresses.

Is there a separate tax on Bitcoin UK?

There is not a separate universal Bitcoin tax rate. The treatment depends on whether a transaction produces a chargeable gain, taxable income or neither.
