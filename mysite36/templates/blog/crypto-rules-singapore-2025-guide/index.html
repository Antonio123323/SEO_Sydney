Crypto rules Singapore residents encounter are not a single ban or blanket approval of digital assets. The relevant requirements depend on what a company actually does: exchanging Digital Payment Tokens (DPTs), arranging transfers, safeguarding customer assets, providing investment services or operating a wallet interface. A regulated service is not the same as a guaranteed investment.

This guide explains cryptocurrency regulations Singapore users and businesses should understand, including the Payment Services Act, MAS licensing checks, AML and customer due diligence, the separate digital token service provider regime and retail-user safeguards. It focuses on regulation rather than repeating our guides on tax treatment, buying crypto or trading platforms.

What Do Singapore Crypto Rules Cover?

Singapore regulates covered financial services rather than treating every blockchain product identically. A cryptocurrency exchange, a digital token custodian and a self-custody wallet application can have different obligations. The category of token and the precise regulated activity matter more than a product name or an overseas badge.

For readers assessing MAS crypto compliance Singapore requirements, the first practical step is to identify the contractual legal entity and the service it offers. A provider may be authorised for a particular activity without being authorised for every feature advertised on its website.

Which Laws Regulate Digital Tokens?

The Payment Services Act 2019 (PS Act) is the main framework for covered payment services, including Digital Payment Token services. Its scope was expanded in April 2024, including certain digital token custody and transfer-related activities. Depending on the token and activity, the Securities and Futures Act or Financial Advisers Act may also be relevant.

A distinct Financial Services and Markets Act 2022 regime applies to certain Singapore-based Digital Token Service Providers (DTSPs) serving customers solely outside Singapore. From 30 June 2025, covered overseas-only businesses require a licence; MAS has indicated a high licensing bar. This does not mean that every issuer, software wallet or utility token is covered by the same regime.

MAS Crypto Licensing: Who Needs Authorisation?

Crypto licensing Singapore requirements depend on the business model and applicable thresholds. Payment-service providers may operate under a Standard Payment Institution (SPI) or Major Payment Institution (MPI) licence, or another applicable legal status. MAS evaluates the entity, its financial and operational arrangements, governance, security and compliance controls.

Do not assume a parent company licence covers a similarly named website. Check the exact entity in the MAS Financial Institutions Directory, confirm the activity is “Digital Payment Token Service” where relevant and compare the official record with the company’s own legal disclosures.

Digital Payment Token Rules for Consumers

Digital payment token rules Singapore retail users encounter include risk disclosures, measures intended to reduce speculative inducements, limits on certain financing incentives and safeguarding obligations for covered providers. These requirements developed in stages; they should not be described as a single set of new rules introduced in 2026.

Safeguarding rules for regulated providers do not insure a token against price declines or guarantee recovery if users lose keys or approve fraudulent transfers. The custody model, withdrawal conditions and complaint process remain material.

AML, KYC and the Crypto Travel Rule

MAS Notice PSN02 addresses anti-money-laundering and countering-the-financing-of-terrorism obligations for covered DPT service providers. The requirements include customer due diligence, risk assessment, record keeping, transaction monitoring and suspicious transaction reporting. MAS also published supervisory expectations for DPT providers in July 2026.

Crypto KYC requirements Singapore users encounter therefore vary by service. A regulated exchange may request identity and source-of-funds evidence; generating a private key in a self-custody wallet is not, by itself, the same kind of onboarding. Certain transfers can require originator and beneficiary information under applicable requirements, sometimes described as the crypto Travel Rule.

Stablecoins, Wallets and Token Classification

Digital Payment Tokens, asset-backed tokens, capital-markets products and utility or governance tokens may fall under different rules. Stablecoin arrangements have their own regulatory considerations; not every dollar-pegged token is a “MAS-regulated stablecoin”. Users should read the issuer’s redemption terms and identify the legal entity rather than assuming all stablecoins share the same safeguards.

Likewise, self-custody software should not automatically be called “MAS-approved”. A wallet can be a tool for signing blockchain transactions, while integrated exchange, brokerage or payment functions can be supplied by separate entities with their own obligations.

Green Capstead: Compliance Checks Before Registration

Readers comparing digital-asset services may encounter Green Capstead. The provider’s website describes trading-oriented features, but a website description does not independently establish MAS authorisation or confirm availability to Singapore users. Before registering or sending funds, verify the operator and exact service through independent records.

  • Registration: confirm the legal entity, contractual terms and any identity-verification process before sharing documents or creating an account.
  • Fees: request the full written schedule, including trading spreads, funding charges, inactivity fees and withdrawals; do not rely on a general “low fee” claim.
  • Network selection: obtain current instructions for the exact token, network, deposit address and any memo/tag; do not infer network support from a token symbol.
  • Withdrawals and custody: confirm who controls assets, whether fiat withdrawal is supported, applicable restrictions and the verified support channel.

How to Check a Crypto Provider in Singapore

The following sequence is more reliable than trusting an advertised compliance seal:

  • Write down the company’s exact registered legal name from its terms, not only the website brand.
  • Search the MAS Financial Institutions Directory and inspect the licence or exemption and authorised activities.
  • Check that the website, entity name, contractual counterparty and payment recipient actually match.
  • Review custody, risk disclosure, complaints, withdrawal limits, fees and account verification requirements.
  • Avoid transferring funds if a provider claims a MAS licence that you cannot match to the entity and activity.

Common Regulatory Misunderstandings

  • “All crypto companies must hold the same MAS licence.” False: activities and token classifications determine the applicable regime.
  • “A MAS-listed company guarantees a safe investment.” False: authorisation does not remove volatility, fraud or technical risk.
  • “A licence for one service covers the operator’s every product.” Not necessarily: compare the exact permitted activities.
  • “A self-custody wallet automatically needs the same KYC process as an exchange.” Not necessarily: a software tool and a regulated intermediary are different.
  • “A business serving only overseas customers can ignore Singapore rules.” Not necessarily: certain Singapore-based overseas-only DTSP activities fall under FSMA requirements.

FAQ

Are cryptocurrencies legal in Singapore?

Holding or using crypto is not generally prohibited, but providers conducting regulated activities may require MAS authorisation. The legal treatment depends on the service and token.

What is a Digital Payment Token service?

It is a category of payment services under Singapore law covering specified activities involving Digital Payment Tokens. The relevant scope should be checked against the current Payment Services Act and MAS guidance.

Do crypto exchanges need a MAS licence?

Covered DPT service providers generally need the applicable authorisation or legal exemption. Always verify the exact legal entity and activity.

What is the difference between PS Act and FSMA digital token rules?

The PS Act governs covered payment services, including DPT services. The separate FSMA DTSP regime addresses specified Singapore-based providers serving customers solely outside Singapore.

Does a MAS licence guarantee customer funds?

No. Safeguarding and conduct requirements do not eliminate token-price losses, cyber risks or every operational failure.

Do Singapore users always need KYC for a crypto wallet?

A self-custody wallet can generate keys locally, but regulated trading, custody and fiat services may require customer due diligence.

Does MAS approve every stablecoin?

No. Issuer status, token structure and the specific regulatory framework must be checked individually.

Conclusion

Singapore cryptocurrency regulation is activity-based. Check the provider’s exact entity, relevant MAS authorisation, customer-asset arrangements and the practical details of each service before using it. Understand the difference between a payment-token provider, a capital-markets service and a self-custody tool. Compliance is an important check, not an assurance of investment returns.
