Anyone looking to avoid crypto scams in Singapore should learn to recognise the warning signs before creating an account, sharing personal information or transferring digital assets. A polished website, familiar logo or persuasive account manager can be part of a fraudulent investment scheme. Even an apparently successful test withdrawal does not prove that later deposits are safe.

This guide explains how fake trading platforms operate, how to check a provider independently, and what to do if cryptocurrency has already been sent. It focuses on scam detection rather than wallet rankings or investment returns. Digital assets can involve major market losses even when a service is legitimate.

How Crypto Investment Scams Work

A common crypto investment scam begins with an advertisement, dating-app conversation, direct message or investment chat group. The victim is encouraged to open an account on a convincing platform, buy crypto through a genuine service and transfer it to an address provided by the scammer. The fraudulent dashboard may then show invented profits. When the victim tries to withdraw, the operators demand another payment for tax, verification or account release.

The Singapore Police Force has also warned about impersonators posing as government officials or trading-platform support staff. No credible authority should require you to send cryptocurrency to a private wallet for an investigation or to prove that your funds are safe.

How to Spot Fake Crypto Investment Platforms

To spot fake crypto investment platforms, verify the company behind the service before considering any deposit. Look for a legal entity name, jurisdiction, relevant authorised activity, clear risk disclosures, fee schedules and a usable withdrawal policy. Search for contradictions between the website, app listing, payment recipient and the registered operator.

1. Verify the exact legal entity

Check the MAS Financial Institutions Directory for the entity and activity being claimed. The MAS Investor Alert List is useful but not exhaustive: absence from the list does not establish legitimacy. A company name that resembles a licensed institution may be an impersonation.

2. Verify the website and support channels

Type the official domain directly or use a previously verified bookmark. Check lookalike spelling, unusual subdomains, unsolicited installation instructions and whether supposed customer support moves the conversation to an unrelated messaging account. A fake crypto trading platform may copy legitimate branding.

3. Test claims, not just the interface

Ask how assets are held, what withdrawal conditions apply, which legal entity receives funds, and whether the offered service is actually available in Singapore. An apparent balance, review, app-store presence or small initial payout cannot establish that a platform is safe.

Common Cryptocurrency Scam Scenarios

Fake profits and blocked withdrawals

Fraudsters may display fabricated trading gains, then request extra “tax”, compliance or release payments before processing withdrawal. Do not assume that paying one more charge will unlock the balance.

Impersonation of authorities and exchanges

A caller may claim to represent MAS, the police, a bank or an exchange and instruct you to move tokens into a “safe” wallet. Stop the conversation and independently contact the real organisation using an official published channel.

Phishing, malicious approvals and fake applications

Crypto phishing scams Singapore users face may involve replica login pages, counterfeit wallet apps, malicious browser extensions or deceptive smart-contract approvals. Never type a seed phrase into a website in response to a message. Review transaction details and permissions before signing.

Recovery-agent fraud

After an initial loss, another fraudster may promise recovery in exchange for a deposit, wallet access or a “blockchain investigation” charge. A crypto recovery scam can compound the original loss.

How to Avoid Cryptocurrency Scams Before Sending Funds

A straightforward cryptocurrency scam prevention routine is to verify the person, legal entity, destination and transaction separately. Avoid cryptocurrency scams Singapore readers encounter by taking these checks in order:

  • Pause after any unsolicited message or investment invitation; do not follow an artificial deadline.
  • Check the provider and exact activity in the MAS directory; compare details with its official contact information.
  • Use ScamShield to review suspicious messages and links, and call its helpline on 1799 if uncertain.
  • Never share a seed phrase, private key, password or one-time authentication code.
  • Check the full recipient address and blockchain network before a legitimate transfer; irreversible transactions are difficult to recover.
  • Use unique passwords, enable two-factor authentication where available, and monitor account alerts.
  • Keep records of the domain, advertisements, conversations and transaction hashes if something appears suspicious.

Green Capstead: Verify the Provider and Its Claims

If you come across Green Capstead while comparing digital-asset services, apply the same checks you would use for any unfamiliar platform. A website describing automated strategies or investment tools does not independently establish local authorisation, profitability, custody arrangements or withdrawal availability.

Before registering, independently confirm the operating entity and any relevant MAS authorisation. Read the registration conditions and never disclose a wallet recovery phrase or password to a representative. Request the full fee schedule, including trading spreads, account charges, deposits, withdrawals and any minimum balance. Before transferring assets, check the exact token, blockchain network, recipient address and withdrawal rules. Do not send funds until the service and payment recipient have been independently verified.

No mention of a brand in this guide is a certification or endorsement. If the legal entity, fees or payout terms cannot be verified, do not assume the platform is suitable for your transaction.

What to Do If You Have Already Sent Crypto

If you suspect a crypto investment scam Singapore authorities recommend acting immediately. A confirmed blockchain transfer may not be reversible, but timely reporting and account restrictions may reduce further loss.

  • Stop making payments, including supposed withdrawal taxes, verification charges or recovery deposits.
  • Contact the exchange or wallet provider through its verified support channel and ask whether the account can be restricted or further withdrawals halted.
  • If your recovery phrase or private key was exposed, create a fresh wallet on a trusted device and move any remaining assets to an independently verified address.
  • Review and revoke suspicious token approvals when the wallet and network support that action.
  • Contact your bank immediately if fiat transfers or cards were involved; secure email and account credentials.
  • Record wallet addresses, transaction hashes, screenshots, timestamps, domains and chat histories.
  • Make a police report and seek advice through ScamShield; do not rely on unsolicited “asset recovery” offers.

Singapore Reporting and Regulatory Checks

The Monetary Authority of Singapore supervises specified Digital Payment Token service activities, but regulation is not an assurance of investment performance or full reimbursement after fraud. The Financial Institutions Directory helps verify regulated entities and their permitted services. The Investor Alert List is not a complete register of every scam or impersonator.

ScamShield offers checks and a Singapore helpline at 1799. If funds or credentials have been compromised, contact the relevant bank or crypto provider promptly and report suspected criminal activity to the Singapore Police Force. Keep copies of evidence without publishing private recovery details.

The Singapore Police Force warned in March 2026 that cryptocurrency losses in 2025 were driven in part by investment, job and government-impersonation scams. More recent consumer guidance also highlights fake advertisements and investment-chat groups. These patterns are reasons to verify independently rather than trust a polished presentation.

FAQ

How can I avoid crypto scams in Singapore?

Check the legal entity and relevant activity in official MAS records, independently verify contact details, reject guaranteed-return promises and never share wallet recovery credentials.

What are the clearest signs of a fake crypto trading platform?

Unverifiable operator details, guaranteed profits, pressure to deposit, invented account balances and demands for new payments before withdrawals are major red flags.

Can a MAS licence guarantee that my crypto investment is safe?

No. Check the exact regulated activity, but remember that token prices, scams and operational failures can still lead to losses.

What should I do if a platform asks for a withdrawal release fee?

Pause and independently verify the claim. Do not send additional cryptocurrency to a suspicious operator solely to release a purported balance.

Can the police reverse a cryptocurrency transfer?

Confirmed on-chain transfers are often irreversible. Report promptly, preserve transaction evidence and contact the provider in case any further transfers can be stopped.

Is an app safe because it appears in an official app store?

No. App-store presence alone does not establish a legitimate operator, safe investment offering or appropriate local authorisation.

How do I report a crypto scam in Singapore?

Contact the relevant bank or exchange, make a police report and use ScamShield resources. The ScamShield helpline is 1799.

Conclusion

The best way to avoid crypto scams in Singapore is to verify before you transfer. Fake investment platforms rely on convincing presentation, time pressure and misleading profit claims; the most useful safeguards are independent operator checks, careful wallet security and a refusal to pay unexpected release fees. If you have already sent funds, stop further payments, secure your accounts, preserve evidence and contact the appropriate service providers and authorities promptly.
